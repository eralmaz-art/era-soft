# ERA Construction Business Rules

Status: **Draft — Business Model gate**<br>
Scope: **ERA Construction**<br>
Authority: **Business rules only; no implementation authorization**

## Purpose

This document is the business constitution of ERA Construction. It defines the
conditions that must remain true regardless of future screens, records,
automation, or accounting mechanisms. The rules protect accountability,
traceability, project control, and trustworthy management information.

A rule may be changed only through an explicit product and process-owner
decision. Local convenience, a software limitation, or an existing habit is not
enough to bypass it.

## Rule Interpretation

- **Must** indicates a mandatory condition.
- **May** indicates a controlled business option.
- **Exception** means an explicitly approved departure with a reason, owner,
  consequence, evidence, and resolution date. It never means silent bypass.
- A business event is not proven merely because a document exists; the required
  evidence and accountable confirmation must also exist.
- Where one person holds several roles, required independent review still
  applies whenever segregation of duties demands it.

## 1. Accountability and Authority

**BR-GOV-001 — Accountable owner.** Every material need, purchase, acceptance,
budget change, obligation, payment, and exception must have one accountable
business owner. Shared participation does not remove single accountability.

**BR-GOV-002 — Explicit authority.** Approval authority must be defined by the
relevant company, object, decision type, value, and risk. Visibility or job
title alone does not grant authority.

**BR-GOV-003 — Independent approval.** A person must not approve their own
request where independent review is required. Role overlap in a small team must
be resolved by an authorized alternate, not by removing the control.

**BR-GOV-004 — Decision context.** Every approval must preserve the inputs,
reason, decision maker, time, outcome, conditions, and known consequence that
formed the decision.

**BR-GOV-005 — Controlled exception.** An exception must identify the rule being
departed from, the reason, supporting evidence, approver, risk owner, expiry or
resolution condition, and business consequence.

**BR-GOV-006 — No silent overwrite.** Approved facts and decisions must be
changed only by an explicit revision, correction, cancellation, or reversal
that preserves history.

## 2. Project Context and Lifecycle

**BR-PRJ-001 — One operating context.** Every operational purchase, delivery,
accepted service, cost, obligation, and payment must belong to one company and
one construction object. A genuinely shared item must use an explicit approved
allocation; it must not be assigned to an artificial object merely to complete
processing.

**BR-PRJ-002 — Responsible project leadership.** An object must have an
accountable Project Manager and defined decision authorities before commercial
commitments or site execution begin.

**BR-PRJ-003 — Approved project state.** Business actions must be consistent
with the object's approved lifecycle state. Starting, suspending, resuming, or
closing an object requires an explicit business decision.

**BR-PRJ-004 — Sufficient cost purpose.** Each material business event must
carry the project, work, and cost context required to explain why the value was
incurred. Missing context remains visible and owned until corrected; it is not
silently excluded from management reporting.

**BR-PRJ-005 — Project closeout.** An object may close only when open
commitments, payables, advances, retentions, claims, stock, accepted work,
required documents, and forecast consequences are reconciled or explicitly
accepted as residual exceptions.

## 3. Budget and Forecast Control

**BR-BUD-001 — Approved initial baseline.** Each object has one approved initial
budget baseline. Once approved, it remains visible and is not rewritten.

**BR-BUD-002 — Revision history.** Every budget revision must state the changed
scope or assumption, reason, impact, effective date, owner, and approval. The
current authorized budget must be distinguishable from all prior versions.

**BR-BUD-003 — Exception is not revision.** Permission to proceed outside the
current budget is a budget exception, not a budget revision. It does not alter
the authorized baseline unless a separate revision is approved.

**BR-BUD-004 — No retroactive authorization.** A later budget revision must not
silently legitimize an earlier unauthorized decision. The original exception
and its consequence remain traceable.

**BR-BUD-005 — Budget check supports approval.** Budget availability informs a
purchase, contract, or payment decision but never replaces the accountable
approval.

**BR-BUD-006 — Complete visibility.** Unbudgeted, uncoded, disputed, or
incomplete costs and commitments must remain visible. Missing classification is
an exception to resolve, not a reason to omit the value.

**BR-BUD-007 — Forecast ownership.** The Project Manager owns the current view
of expected project completion cost and its assumptions. Finance, procurement,
and cost control own the source facts they contribute.

## 4. Need and Procurement

**BR-PRC-001 — Need before commitment.** A purchase begins with a defined
business need containing an object, purpose, responsible owner, required date,
quantity or scope, and cost context.

**BR-PRC-002 — Approval before commitment.** A supplier or contractor must not
receive a commercial commitment before the required need, budget, authority,
and commercial approvals exist, except through an approved emergency path.

**BR-PRC-003 — Evidence of supplier choice.** Supplier or contractor selection
must retain the comparison, qualification, prior agreement, or approved
sole-source reason that supports the choice.

**BR-PRC-004 — Defined commercial obligation.** Every purchase must identify an
owner, supplier or contractor, deliverable, quantity or scope, price basis,
required dates, acceptance conditions, payment terms, and applicable contract
or exception.

**BR-PRC-005 — Materials and services remain distinct.** Material supply and
service or subcontracted work may share approval and payment control, but each
must use evidence appropriate to what is being accepted.

**BR-PRC-006 — Commercial change control.** Changes, cancellations, and claims
must state their reason and consequences for scope, budget, commitment,
delivery, payment, and schedule.

**BR-PRC-007 — Delivery promise ownership.** Every outstanding supply promise
must have a responsible follow-up owner and a required-on-site date against
which risk can be assessed.

## 5. Material Delivery and Control

**BR-MAT-001 — Authorized receipt.** A material delivery must be confirmed by an
authorized receiver against the expected supply and must state accepted,
rejected, damaged, excess, short, and pending quantities as applicable.

**BR-MAT-002 — Receipt is not consumption.** Arrival or warehouse receipt proves
custody of accepted material; it does not prove use in construction work.

**BR-MAT-003 — Transfer is not consumption.** Moving material between locations
changes custody or location, not the amount consumed by the object.

**BR-MAT-004 — Traceable movement.** Every material movement must identify the
material, quantity, date, origin, destination, object, purpose, and accountable
custodian.

**BR-MAT-005 — Consumption evidence.** Material consumption must be supported by
the work or site purpose, quantity, date, responsible person, and required
confirmation.

**BR-MAT-006 — Discrepancy ownership.** Shortage, damage, substitution, quality
failure, and unexplained balance must remain open with an owner and resolution
path until accepted, replaced, returned, recovered, or written off by authority.

**BR-MAT-007 — Explicit return and correction.** Supplier returns, site returns,
and quantity corrections are separate traceable events and must preserve their
effect on custody, obligation, and project cost.

## 6. Service and Work Acceptance

**BR-SVC-001 — Evidence before acceptance.** Service or subcontracted work must
be measured against the agreed scope, period, quantity, quality, milestones,
and acceptance conditions before it is treated as accepted.

**BR-SVC-002 — Authorized acceptance.** Only a role with project and technical
authority may accept completed work. Commercial or finance roles may validate
terms but do not replace technical acceptance.

**BR-SVC-003 — Invoice is not proof of work.** A supplier invoice alone does not
prove that a service or construction work was performed or accepted.

**BR-SVC-004 — Partial and disputed work.** Accepted, rejected, disputed,
incomplete, and pending-correction quantities or value must remain
distinguishable until resolution.

**BR-SVC-005 — Acceptance consequence.** Acceptance must make visible the
resulting obligation, project-cost, forecast, quality, and schedule consequence
before downstream approval.

## 7. Invoice, Obligation, and Payment Control

**BR-FIN-001 — Supported obligation.** A supplier invoice must be supported by
the relevant commercial terms and accepted material or service evidence, or by
an explicit authorized exception.

**BR-FIN-002 — Reconciliation before approval.** Quantity, price, terms,
acceptance, prior payments, advances, retention, tax evidence, allocation, and
disputes must be understood before an obligation is approved for payment.

**BR-FIN-003 — Payment requires evidence.** Payment requires supporting
commercial and acceptance evidence unless it is explicitly approved as an
advance or another controlled exception.

**BR-FIN-004 — Payment decision is separate.** Validating an obligation does not
automatically authorize when, how, or from which account it will be paid.

**BR-FIN-005 — Payment is not cost recognition.** Cash payment and management
project cost are separate business facts. Neither may be used as an automatic
substitute for the other.

**BR-FIN-006 — Advance remains unresolved.** An advance remains visible until it
is allocated to accepted supply or work, refunded, recovered, or closed through
an explicit authorized resolution.

**BR-FIN-007 — Retention remains explicit.** Retained amounts, their release
conditions, due dates, and responsible owner must remain distinguishable from
ordinary payables and supplier advances.

**BR-FIN-008 — Explicit allocation.** A payment, invoice, or cost covering more
than one object or cost purpose must use an approved allocation that preserves
the total and its rationale.

**BR-FIN-009 — Dispute visibility.** A disputed obligation must remain visible
with the disputed reason, undisputed portion, owner, required action, and cash
consequence.

## 8. Management Information

**BR-INF-001 — Traceable management number.** Every material KPI and management
amount must have a business owner, scope, period, unit or currency, freshness,
source facts, and a path to supporting evidence.

**BR-INF-002 — Distinct control states.** Requested, approved, ordered,
delivered, accepted, invoiced, committed, actual, paid, payable, and forecast
values must remain distinguishable even when shown together.

**BR-INF-003 — No false completeness.** Missing, stale, unclassified, disputed,
or unreconciled information must be labelled as such. It must not be displayed
as zero, complete, or safe without evidence.

**BR-INF-004 — Consistent cut-off.** A management view must identify the date and
business cut-off represented so that different decisions are not compared
across incompatible periods.

**BR-INF-005 — Reconciliation ownership.** Differences between procurement,
site, finance, cash, and project-control facts must have an accountable owner
and resolution status.

**BR-INF-006 — Decision-oriented reporting.** A report or KPI must support a
named business decision, control, or audit requirement. Information without a
defined use is not part of the core product by default.

## 9. Evidence, Time, and Auditability

**BR-AUD-001 — Evidence follows the event.** Supporting evidence must be linked
to the object, business event, and decision it proves; storing a file without
that context is insufficient.

**BR-AUD-002 — Decision history.** Material decisions must preserve actor,
authority, timestamp, inputs, outcome, conditions, reason, and later reversal or
supersession.

**BR-AUD-003 — Authoritative status.** Each business fact has one authoritative
current status and a preserved status history. Conflicting manual status lists
are not permitted as sources of truth.

**BR-AUD-004 — Deadline meaning.** Required dates, due dates, ageing, pauses,
and escalations must use an agreed business calendar and clearly assigned
responsibility.

**BR-AUD-005 — Appropriate confidentiality.** Access to financial, payroll,
bank, contract, supplier, and owner information must follow company, object,
role, and decision responsibility without weakening traceability.

## Controlled Exceptions

An exception is acceptable only when normal processing would create a greater
business risk or when an authorized policy explicitly permits it. At minimum it
must record:

- the normal rule and requested departure;
- the business reason and urgency;
- the object, value, scope, and duration affected;
- supporting evidence available and evidence still missing;
- the decision maker and risk owner;
- the budget, cash, schedule, quality, and control consequences;
- the resolution action and due date;
- the final outcome.

Repeated exceptions of the same type require a process-owner review. They are
evidence of a policy or operating-model problem, not a permanent alternative
process.

## Approval Requirements

Before this document can become **Business Model Approved**:

1. The Product Owner and named process owners must be confirmed.
2. Each rule must have an accountable process owner.
3. Approval authorities, value thresholds, and required independent reviews
   must be decided.
4. Material/service acceptance, management cost, advances, retentions,
   emergency purchasing, and closeout rules must be validated on a real pilot.
5. Any approved deviation from these rules must be recorded with its rationale.
